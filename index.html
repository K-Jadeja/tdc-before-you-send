1. How a match gets made: the matchmaker sends a client the profile of a possible match. If the client says yes, the matchmaker asks the match. Only when both say yes do they meet.
  2. In the brief, 1,000 profiles were sent. The client said yes 310 times, the match said yes back 210 times, and 42 pairs actually met.
  3. 690 were turned down. 242 of those broke a rule the client had already given. Claude's answer: build a rule checker. Krishna: every AI will say that.
  4. 448 rejections had no rule behind them. Krishna asks what clients are not telling us: they turn someone down, then accept someone almost the same.
  5. Krishna's idea was to learn each client's type. A test on 8,368 real decisions from a Columbia University speed-dating study showed a person's own past choices predicted a yes worse than general appeal did. The idea lost, and was parked.
  6. What the data left standing: the same person gave a near-identical match a different answer 33% of the time and 63% of yeses were never returned (both from a Columbia University speed-dating study); 24% of sends in the brief broke a rule the client had already given.
  7. The answer: before a profile is sent, the matchmaker sees three facts. Does the profile break a deal breaker? Will the other person say yes back? Has the client refused someone similar? No score. The matchmaker decides.
  8. Today 21 in 100 sends end in a mutual yes. The goal for month one is 25 in 100.

Before you send

Only 21 of every 100 profiles sent end in a mutual yes. Three checks at the moment of sending would catch the avoidable ones.

Three checks, no score

Client: the person The Date Crew is finding a partner for. Match: the person whose profile is being sent to them.

1. What the client already told us

Does this profile break a deal breaker, or only a softer preference?

"Don't send. This client said no smokers."

2. Will the match say yes back?

A meeting needs two yeses. If this match rarely says yes back, ask them first, so the client is never let down.

"Three clients said yes to him. He said yes to none. Ask him first."

3. The client's earlier answers

Has this client already refused, or accepted, someone similar?

"This client passed on a similar profile in week one, and has accepted two like it since."

In order, cheapest first

  1. A process change, this week, no code. Pin each client's deal breakers at the top of their thread and measure for a week. If that fixes it, part of the tool is unnecessary.
  2. A side panel in Gmail. Their email already runs on Gmail, so the matchmaker opens nothing new. It shows the three checks above, plus a second-look list of profiles a client passed on without a specific reason that resemble ones they accepted later.
  3. Not yet: a per-client taste model. I tested it on real choice data and it lost, so it waits until The Date Crew's own data says otherwise.

What should move

21% → 25%mutual-yes rate per profile sent, within a month. 27.7% is the ceiling from removing avoidable sends alone.
42 → 55completed meetings a month if avoidable sends are replaced with compliant ones and later steps hold.
